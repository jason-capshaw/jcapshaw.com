## Business challenge

B2B ordering carried a lot of dead time. Customers were copying SKUs from paper, calling reps to confirm availability, and waiting on quotes that should have been transactional. The sales relationship was load-bearing in ways it shouldn't have been: it was compensating for a buying experience that had never really been designed.

## Approach

The brief was to build a customer-first ordering experience, not a digital catalog. That meant starting from how customers actually placed orders (mobile, on a warehouse floor, with a scanner in one hand) and working backwards into the platform.

Warehouse-systems integration ran underneath, so inventory, availability, and pricing were live rather than batched. Mobile scanning let buyers move through a real or expected order without keystrokes. Rapid reordering treated recurring purchases as first-class, not a feature buried inside account settings. Sales teams kept their role, but it shifted from order taking to consultative work the system couldn't do.

## Impact

Customer ordering time fell by roughly 60% on representative flows. Engagement and stickiness improved as the platform absorbed friction that sales teams had been compensating for. The architectural pattern (B2B-aware commerce on a flexible storefront) was later commercialized by a partner and became part of a major commerce platform's B2B offering.
