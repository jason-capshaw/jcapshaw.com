## Business challenge

Executive teams at enterprise distributors usually know the competitors by name but don't have a structured read of where they actually stand. Decisions about where to invest get made on instinct, recent customer anecdotes, or whichever competitor's earnings call was loudest. That is not a strategy.

## Approach

Built the benchmarking work as a system. Defined the dimensions that mattered (assortment depth, digital capability, service levels, fulfillment options, pricing posture, account programs), then collected comparable data on the competitive set against each dimension. Gap analysis turned that data into a sortable view of where the business led, matched, or trailed.

Growth-opportunity work started from the gaps. Each opportunity was sized for both the addressable upside and the cost to close, so the executive conversation had a structured shape rather than a list of ideas.

## Impact

Executive teams had a defensible read of where they stood across product, service, and digital capability. Investment decisions could be made against a clear thesis rather than instinct or the last conversation. Strategic planning and market positioning work became repeatable rather than ad hoc.
