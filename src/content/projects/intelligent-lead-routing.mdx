## Business challenge

Lead-generation businesses tend to optimize for volume and call it customer acquisition. That works until customers compare experiences. A buyer who waits forty minutes for the first provider response remembers the wait more than they remember the brand that referred them.

## Approach

Built routing as a matching problem, not a distribution problem. Inputs were the inbound customer (intent, location, urgency) and the provider pool (capacity, recent response time, demonstrated fit for the job). The system scored both sides and routed to the best available match inside a tight time window.

SEO and paid search supplied qualified inbound demand, but the routing layer was where customer experience was won or lost. Response-time optimization was treated as a product surface, not an operations dashboard.

## Impact

Lead handoff moved from round-robin distribution to context-aware matching. Customer experience became the optimization target instead of marketing throughput, and that reframing changed which metrics the acquisition team watched daily.
